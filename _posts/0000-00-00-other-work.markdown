## Qualitative Research
### Teachers' Perspectives on Technologies for Science Labs
![Survey results from science teachers]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/mega.png)
To find out what science labs actually need, I went to the people who run them. I visited 7 schools, surveyed 58 teachers, and interviewed 18 teachers across a wide range of socioeconomic contexts.

The clearest need was that teachers want technology to **augment hands-on labs, not replace them**. Students learn the most by handling real equipment and working through messy, real data, but most lab technology swaps the experiment for a "perfect" simulation. That finding became the motivation for my research.

**Tools & techniques:** Needfinding, classroom observation, surveys, semi-structured interviews, and qualitative analysis.

## Research and Industry Collaborations
### Augmented and Virtual Reality for Distance Learning
![AR and VR for distance learning]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/TVCG/quiver.png)
*IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 2024*

A first-author journal survey of how AR and VR can support distance learning. It synthesizes prior work on social presence, engagement, comprehension, and instructor tools, and lays out the challenges that remain, from headset comfort to no-code tools that let teachers build their own lessons.

**Tools & techniques:** Literature review and synthesis, AR and VR system analysis, and academic writing.

### Seeing Inside Sealed Robots with AR
*Lam Research · Global Products Engineering*

<!-- <div style="position: relative; padding-bottom: 56.25%; height: 0; overflow: hidden; max-width: 100%;">
  <iframe style="position: absolute; top: 0; left: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%;" src="https://drive.google.com/file/d/18OwcHgKRvxQDop5DaIBVCX3VmvaQQ6bx/preview" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>
</div> -->

Industrial robots often run inside opaque enclosures for safety and to reduce contamination, which makes errors hard to diagnose. HoloLens applications show the robot as a hologram that follows the real robot's motion, turning numbers on a screen into something engineers can see in space. Going beyond the assigned work, a self-initiated internal application visualizes what the robot was doing right before an error, helping technicians pinpoint and diagnose the issue.
<!-- Note the video uses a visible robot to show how the holographic robot can respond to the real robot’s change in movement. -->

**Tools & techniques:** Microsoft HoloLens, AR application development, robot data visualization, and UX design for industrial diagnostics.
