Physics simulations can show forces like lift, tension, and electric fields, but learners can only watch them. KITE lets them feel those forces. It is a free-moving haptic robot that glides across a desk like a mouse and, when it meets a virtual object or force in the simulation, pushes back with multidirectional force and torque that line up with what is on screen. One device supports experiences across physics, from aerodynamics to chemistry.

## Hardware Design
<p align="center">
  <img src="{{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/themes/mechanical-design.webp" alt="Exploded CAD view of KITE showing its camera, motors, omni wheels, axles, wheel retainers, battery, Raspberry Pi, and power converters" width="85%"/>
</p>

- **Gearless actuation.** Gearless motors drive omni wheels, so KITE can move and push in any direction. Custom motor control renders smooth forces and torques without the backlash and friction a geared drive would add.
- **Hybrid localization.** Overhead camera tracking gives KITE's absolute position, and motor feedback fills in between camera updates, keeping force rendering precise and responsive.
- **Self-contained body.** An onboard camera, Raspberry Pi, battery, and buck and boost power converters all fit inside one compact housing.

## Interaction Design
The physical device stays the learner's main way of interacting. KITE moves freely until it meets something in the simulation, and the simulation reacts to where the learner moves it and how hard they push. The same robot supports a range of physics experiences:

- Feeling **lift** on an airfoil
- Pulling apart **molecular bonds**
- Exploring **tidal forces** and **electric fields**
- Stretching and compressing **springs**
- Comparing different forms of **friction**
