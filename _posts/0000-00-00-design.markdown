## KITE: Feeling Simulated Forces
*Microsoft Research*
<div style="position: relative; padding-bottom: 56.25%; height: 0; overflow: hidden; max-width: 100%;">
  <iframe style="position: absolute; top: 0; left: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%;" src="https://www.youtube-nocookie.com/embed/JxwKrtG6HAw?rel=0&start=120" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>
</div>
KITE turns physics simulations into something you can feel. It glides across the desk like a mouse until it meets a virtual object or force, then pushes back in line with what's on screen. Learners can feel lift on an airfoil, pull a molecular bond apart, stretch a spring, and compare different kinds of friction with their own hands.

**Tools & techniques:** Haptic rendering, force feedback, simulation integration, and interaction design.

## Smartphone-Enabled Microscope
<p align="center">
  <img src="{{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/microscope/micro.png" alt="Smartphone-enabled microscope" width="40%"/>
</p>

A low-cost microscope built from 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf electronics. Its real focus knob, objective turret, and slide stage are instrumented with encoders, magnetic detents, and potentiometers, so turning them drives high-resolution imagery on a connected smartphone. Students keep the physical habits of microscopy without the cost and fragility of lab optics.

**Tools & techniques:** Sensor integration, syncing hardware with a mobile app, 3D printing, and designing for low cost.

## Mario vs. Sonic: A Mechatronic Tabletop Game
<div style="position: relative; padding-bottom: 56.25%; height: 0; overflow: hidden; max-width: 100%;">
  <iframe style="position: absolute; top: 0; left: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/e7sFuR1ro0Q" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen></iframe>
</div>
Mario and Sonic face off in a tabletop hockey game where the play is physical but the game is digital. Using buttons, players move and swing physical hockey sticks across the board, and beam-break sensors catch every goal so the electronics can keep score and react in real time. The mechanism and the game logic were designed together, so each physical play becomes a game event.

**Tools & techniques:** Arduino programming, servo actuation, beam-break sensing, 3D printing, and linking physical play to game logic.
